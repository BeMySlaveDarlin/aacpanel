A session is a conversation

The feed shows it as it happens, with the composer under the thumb; a team of agents reads as one thread, each of them named.

The sheets: a question to answer, the calls behind an answer

A question from the session opens as a sheet and is answered with one tap; the tool calls behind a message open the same way, each with its result.

The cards in the feed: a permission to grant, a letter to read

When Claude Code asks whether it may run a command, the same three answers come to the page — numbered, with “from now on” where it offers to remember. A subagent's letter lands in the same feed, and unfolds.

Subagents, and the archive

Every subagent has a page of its own, with the report it returned; a closed conversation keeps its thread and comes back with one tap.

Before anything runs, the panel names the consequence

Containers stand by stack. Each action says what it will cost first, and the journal keeps who pressed it and how it ended.

The session's own terminal

The same tmux the session runs in, typed into from the page, one tab further than the feed.

The load of the host, and the usage broken down

Processor, memory, disks and the top processes on one screen; the transcripts summed by day, model, tool and account on the next.

Three processes, and the line between them is the point

The panel asks; something else decides. That line is why it can be opened from outside at all.

The service in its container asks; the executor on the host carries out one of twenty-two named actions; the collector only reads. ARCHITECTURE.md holds the boundaries.

About half an hour on a fresh machine

What it needs first: a Linux machine with systemd and docker, Go, tmux and python3, and a claude you have already signed in to. Docker brings up the service and its Postgres; the executor you build yourself, so the one piece allowed to touch the machine arrives as your own binary.

docker compose up -d
go build ./cmd/aacpanel-exec
systemctl --user start aacpanel-exec

Three lines are the shape of it, not the whole: it is eighteen steps, and the order is the point — the machine description is read at startup by all three processes, the database comes before the role that uses it. INSTALL.md is written to be followed from the top down.

The way in is a code the binary prints on the machine itself, and there is no other one. It lives five minutes, which is time enough to put a passkey on the phone you will actually use; after that the phone is the key. Name a profile, a group and a project on the first screen, and the sessions of that machine are yours to answer.

One person, one machine, used there every day

Chrome and Chromium browsers, on Android and on the desk. Issues are welcome.

There is no price list, because there is nothing to sell: no account, no plan, no server of ours between you and your machine. The panel runs on your hardware, the licence is MIT, and that is the whole of it.
